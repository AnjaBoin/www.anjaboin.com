For years I had been playing with the idea of starting my own website. Websites have fascinated me for as long as I can remember. Especially the beautiful, polished ones, where everything is in exactly the right place and you almost automatically want to keep clicking. I wanted to be able to do that too. Not just imagine what something like that should look like, but ideally build it myself.<br />For the Oldies among us: in my time, there was Altavista instead of Google.......

## Learning to code

My chance came in the Covid year. I discovered the subsidised programme *NL leert door* (which roughly means *the Netherlands keeps learning*), through which the Dutch government offered all kinds of free courses. A fantastic opportunity to really let my curiosity run free. I completed several courses (in practice, that meant grabbing everything I could: I finished 52 courses with a certificate) and, to my great delight, was even able to train as a Full Stack Developer. That sounded quite impressive straight away. And complicated. But I found that out soon enough.

The course was inspiring, but also somewhat sobering. I found a study buddy in Sander, who started at the same time as me. There was just one small difference: Sander was thirty years younger than me. We were both equally motivated and driven, but while he sometimes seemed to soak up the material before the teacher had even finished speaking, I occasionally needed a bit more time to let it sink in. And then let it sink in again.

## Not getting any younger

Well, and then came the painful realisation: dammit, I'm getting older.

All my life I had learned, studied and picked up new things. Usually it came fairly easily to me. But programming turned out to be a different story. My brain apparently needed time to get used to the idea that it no longer had an instant answer to everything.

After four months of the course I could find my way around PHP, HTML and CSS quite well. A good foundation, but I wasn't there yet. Because although I now knew how to program a website, designing one turned out to require a completely different talent. And that happens to be not exactly my strongest point. My brain isn't really a creative factory that spits out a beautiful design on command. I'm excellent at copying things. During the course we rebuilt the Google website, and that went really well. But coming up with my own website? That was a different kettle of fish. I didn't really know what I wanted, what it should look like and, above all, what I wanted to put on it. And so the website project disappeared back into the familiar drawer of good intentions. Only to stay there comfortably for a few years.

## An unexpected turn

Until my life took an unexpected turn. When health reasons kept me out of the cockpit, I found myself at home and was more or less forced to think about what I still wanted to do with my life. By then I was past fifty and facing quite a career change. If I wanted to take a new professional turn, this was really the moment.

It quickly became clear what I wanted. I wanted to use my rather unusual career and the choices I had made in my life to inspire others. I had been through quite a lot by then. From physiotherapist to pilot, from mediator to a new training in IT: my life had certainly not followed a standard script. Perhaps others could benefit from my experiences, my choices and the lessons I had learned along the way. I also wanted to do more justice to my training and interests in communication, Nonviolent Communication and coaching. Interest in other people and caring for others are deeply rooted in me. Yet in my working life I hadn't often had the chance to really put those qualities centre stage.

## An IT career and a website of my own

By a roundabout route, I eventually ended up at the BFW in Oberhausen (you can read more about that in [this article](/en/blog/grenzarbeiter/). There I chose to retrain as an application developer (Fachinformatikerin für Anwendungsentwicklung). And look at that: suddenly two worlds came together – my old wish to build a website and my new training in IT. During my internship I had some time to spare. And more importantly: I finally had inspiration.

I wanted to create something where others could share in my rather unusual life. My experiences, my choices, my adventures and perhaps my missteps too. Not because my life is so exceptional that it deserves a biography with a print run of a hundred thousand copies, but because I believe we can learn a lot from each other. And sometimes it's simply nice to take a peek into someone else's life. A website seemed the ideal medium for that. Everyone can decide for themselves what they want to read, when and how much. No obligations, no subscription to my daily goings-on and, thankfully, no weekly newsletter you can't unsubscribe from.

What's more, I now had both the time and the opportunity to build my website entirely myself. From scratch, with the knowledge I had gained during my training. This time I also knew much better what I wanted it for. The project had finally found its destination. For the design, I drew inspiration from a few websites I really liked. Brené Brown's website appealed to me with its large photos and its warm, inviting look. With Austin Kleon, on the other hand, I liked the clear and logical structure. I decided to use elements of both websites as inspiration for my own design.

## My website – and what's in it for you?

On my website you will now find, among other things, my career, a detailed story about myself and, of course, my blog. That's where I write about my life, my experiences and the things on my mind. I hope you'll recognise yourself in some of the stories, that you'll smile now and then and perhaps even discover something useful for yourself. A little lightness, a dash of humour and now and then a new perspective: that's what I'd like to give my readers. And who knows, perhaps in this way I can still mean something to others. Not from the cockpit, the massage table or an IT office, but simply from my own website.

So I warmly invite you to have a look around. And I love feedback! What did you enjoy reading? Which story touched you or made you think? And perhaps most importantly: did I manage to inspire you in some way? I'd love to hear from you. Because building a website is something I can now do myself – but a little help finding out what readers think of it is always welcome.
